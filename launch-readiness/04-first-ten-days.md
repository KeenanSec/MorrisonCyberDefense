# First ten business days

Suggested sequence, not an assurance that formation, insurance, or contract review will finish within ten days. Advance only the work whose prerequisites are satisfied.

| When | Keenan — suggested work | Principal — suggested work | Observable result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Review the gap list and contact-channel setup | Confirm actual business status, authority, availability, and initial service | Unknowns have owners; one offer selected |
| Day 2 | Verify the first 10 business contacts and prepare accurate bios | Resolve outreach applicability; initiate necessary contract/insurance/accounting review | Sources recorded; commercial questions assigned |
| Day 3 | Build a labeled lab assessment sample | Review scope, collection method, and quote economics | Rehearsal underway |
| Day 4 | Complete report and another 10 contacts | Review sample, claims, service boundaries, and delivery effort | Approved sales material and credible demonstration |
| Day 5 | Rehearse calls, callback, booking, and follow-up | Review call-ready evidence and availability | Small prospecting batch ready when conditions pass |
| Days 6–7 | Make a manageable batch of manual calls once call-ready; log outcomes | Join qualified discovery calls | Real needs, objections, and dated next actions |
| Day 8 | Draft proposals only for qualified opportunities | Confirm deal-ready conditions and approve each quote | Tailored proposals with supported commitments |
| Day 9 | Follow up as agreed; improve unclear messaging | Address scope/contract questions and capacity | Decisions or clear outstanding blockers |
| Day 10 | Review actual activity and delivery preparation | Decide what to repeat, narrow, or stop | Next-week plan based on observations |

Call-batch size is a capacity decision. Twenty researched prospects is an initial learning sample, not a quota or forecast. If call-readiness is unresolved, continue rehearsal and research instead of treating a calendar date as permission.

## Weekly review: 20 minutes

Discuss conversations held, recurring customer problems, qualified next steps, proposal status, upcoming commitments, and missing readiness evidence. Choose three priorities and assign each one owner. Do not expand into more verticals or buy more tooling until the current offer has been tested in actual conversations.
