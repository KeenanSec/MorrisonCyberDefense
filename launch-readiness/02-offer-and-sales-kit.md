# Proposed first offer and sales kit

Status: founder-review draft. This narrows the existing offering; it does not change approved prices or authorize selling a service you have not rehearsed.

## Choose a manageable first customer

Proposed starting profile: Houston-area commercial contractors with 10–25 Microsoft 365 users, one tenant, an identifiable owner/operations lead, and an existing IT contact. The existing contractor list provides a starting research pool; it does not establish their Microsoft 365 usage or purchasing interest.

Problem to explore: uncertainty about administrative access, email security, and responsibility for resolving security gaps. Ask whether it matters to this business rather than assuming an insurance emergency.

## One-page offer

**Microsoft 365 Security Baseline Review**

We review agreed Microsoft 365 identity and email-security settings and explain the most important observed gaps in business terms. Your existing IT provider can participate and use the resulting action list.

- Scope: one tenant, up to 25 users, and the agreed identity/email settings. No endpoint review, penetration testing, phishing campaign, OT work, or configuration changes in this proposed starter offer.
- Method: authorized read-only review and a short stakeholder interview; exact access and collection method agreed before work.
- Deliverables: concise executive summary, evidence-linked findings with limitations, prioritized remediation backlog, and one findings meeting.
- Proposed fee: **$2,500 fixed**, carried forward from the existing smallest assessment tier. Founder approval and effort/cost validation still required; no claim that the market has validated this price.
- Proposed timing: ten business days after authorization, access, required information, and agreed payment prerequisites are complete; confirm capacity before quoting.
- Proposed payment structure: 50% on acceptance and 50% at the agreed delivery milestone, subject to finalized contract and tax treatment.
- Remediation and ongoing service: separate scope and quote. No automatic subscription or assessment-credit promise.
- No guaranteed prevention of breaches, compliance certification, or insurance acceptance.

Founder decisions before use: exact control coverage, price, payment milestone, contracting identity, turnaround, exclusions, and who is available to review the work.

## Phone opener

“Hi, I'm Keenan with Morrison Cyber Defense. We help small businesses review their Microsoft 365 security settings and turn the findings into a practical action list. Who handles decisions about your business email and IT security?”

If connected to the right person:

“Is this a bad time for a quick question? Do you use Microsoft 365, and is there anything about email security or account access that you would like a clearer picture of?”

If there is a relevant need:

“We offer a fixed-scope review that your existing IT provider can work with. Would a 15-minute conversation with me and our Principal be useful to see whether it fits?”

Only use the service statement after the founders confirm they can deliver it. Do not suggest an assessment has already found an issue. A public lead record grants no permission to scan the company.

## Common responses

- **We have an IT company:** “That makes sense. We can work alongside them on a defined review. Is there a security question you feel is still unanswered?”
- **What does it cost?** After approval: “The starter review is $2,500 for the defined scope. We'd confirm fit before preparing a proposal.” Before approval: explain that pricing follows a scope discussion; do not invent a quote.
- **Send information:** “Sure. What business email should I use, and is there a particular question you'd like the overview to address?” Confirm any requested follow-up.
- **Do you guarantee compliance/insurance?** “No. We document what we observe and recommend actions. Certification and coverage decisions belong to the relevant parties.”
- **Do you have references?** Give only approved, genuine references. If there are none: “We're building the firm's client base. We can show a clearly labeled sample and explain our review process.”
- **Not interested / don't call:** Thank them, end the pitch, and mark the contact do-not-contact.

## Fifteen-minute discovery

1. Why consider this now? What outcome would be useful?
2. How many users and tenants? Microsoft 365 or another platform?
3. Who manages IT, and can that person participate?
4. What is known versus uncertain about admin access, MFA, and email settings?
5. Is there an active suspected incident? If so, route to appropriate response help instead of treating this as a routine review.
6. Who authorizes access and signs the agreement?
7. What deadline, budget process, and availability should the scope reflect?
8. Recap the need and exclusions; agree a dated next step or close as unsuitable.

No passwords, recovery codes, or sensitive exports are needed in discovery. If the prospect cannot fit the scope, propose scoping work or a referral rather than squeezing them into the package.

## Call list and outcomes

Use the blank call-list.csv template in restricted working storage. Research official company websites for published business numbers and buyer roles; record the URL and date. Verify operating status, avoid duplicate businesses/branches, distinguish business lines from personal numbers, and check suppression and applicable outreach rules before calling.

Start with 20 researched accounts. Record actual call attempts, conversations, qualified needs, meetings held, proposals, wins, losses, and next steps. These are learning metrics, not promised conversion targets. A qualified opportunity has a relevant need, scope fit, decision process, and agreed next action.
